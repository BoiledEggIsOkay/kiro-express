TrussLab: help, conventions & method

    

      

        
Quick start

        
    
          
  1. Pick an example from Examples…, or press N and click to place nodes.
    2. 
          
  2. Press M and click node → node to draw members. Clicking empty space creates a node there. Members chain until you press Esc or right-click.
    3. 
          
  3. Press S, choose a support type in the tool bar, and click nodes. Press L, type Fx, Fy, and click nodes to load them.
    4. 
          
  4. With Live on, every edit is re-analysed immediately. Hover a member or node for its results.
    5. 
          
  5. Select an item (V) to edit exact values in the Inspector. Number fields accept expressions such as 2.5*sqrt(3), 12/7 or 10*cos(30) (degrees).
    6.

Units & sign conventions

        
    
          
  • Global axes: x to the right, y upward, angles counter-clockwise from +x in degrees. Coordinates in metres.
    • 
          
  • Loads and reactions are positive in +x / +y. Gravity loads are therefore negative Fy.
    • 
          
  • Axial force, stress and strain are positive in tension and negative in compression.
    • 
          
  • Inputs: E in GPa, σy in MPa, A in mm², I in mm⁴. Force and displacement display units are selectable. All calculations and all exports use SI base units (m, N, Pa).
    • 
        

      

      

        
Supports

        
    
          
  • Pin: ux = uy = 0.
    • 
          
  • Roller (uy = 0): rolls horizontally. Roller (ux = 0): rolls vertically.
    • 
          
  • Inclined roller: rolls on a surface at angle α from +x; the displacement normal to the surface is zero and the reaction acts along that normal. It is handled exactly by rotating the nodal frame (K′ = Λ K Λᵀ), not by a penalty spring. Rotated DOFs are labelled x′, y′.
    • 
          
  • Custom / prescribed: fix x′ and/or y′ of a frame rotated by α and prescribe a displacement on each fixed axis, for example a support settlement δy = −10 mm.
    • 
        

      

      

        
Method: Direct Stiffness Method

        
    
          
  1. For each member: L = hypot(Δx, Δy), c = Δx/L, s = Δy/L (no trigonometric round-off). The global-axis element matrix is
            
    [kᵉ] = (EA/L) ·
              
    c²cs−c²−cs
    css²−cs−s²
    −c²−csc²cs
    −cs−s²css²
    2. 
          
  2. The 2n × 2n global [K] is assembled with Neumaier-compensated summation, so it is exactly symmetric.
    3. 
          
  3. DOFs are split into free (f) and restrained (s): Kff uf = Ff − Kfs us.
    4. 
          
  4. Stability verification before solving: zero-stiffness DOFs are detected, Kff is Jacobi-equilibrated to K̃ = D−½KffD−½, and a rank-revealing (pivoted) Cholesky factorisation tests positive definiteness. A rank deficiency means a mechanism. The solve is then refused and the mechanism mode shape is animated. The Maxwell count m + r − 2j, the determinant (log form, so it cannot overflow), and the 1-norm condition number κ₁ are reported.
    5. 
          
  5. Solver: LU decomposition with partial pivoting, followed by iterative refinement whose residuals are computed with error-free transformations (Dot2) at about twice double precision.
    6. 
          
  6. Reactions: {R} = [K]{u} − {Fext}. Member force: F = (EA/L)[(u₂ − u₁)c + (v₂ − v₁)s]. σ = F/A, ε = ΔL/L.
    7. 
          
  7. Checks: SFyield = σy/|σ|. For compression members SFbuckling = Pcr/|F| with Pcr = π²EI/L². The governing SF is the smaller one. Status is Yielded or Buckled when SF < 1.
    8. 
          
  8. Verification: global equilibrium ΣFx, ΣFy, ΣM, and nodal equilibrium rebuilt from the member forces, are reported in the health panel and the solver log.
    9. 
        

      

      

        
Assumptions & limitations

        
    
          
  • Pin-jointed members with axial force only. Loads act at nodes only. No self-weight, temperature, or geometric/material non-linearity.
    • 
          
  • Linear theory: equilibrium is written in the undeformed geometry. Geometrically unstable configurations (for example three collinear nodes loaded sideways) are reported as mechanisms.
    • 
          
  • Euler buckling assumes ideal pin-ended members (K = 1). If σcr = Pcr/A exceeds σy, yielding governs. Inelastic buckling and code resistance factors are not applied.
    • 
          
  • The Douglas Fir preset uses 49.9 MPa, the clear-wood compressive strength parallel to grain at 12 % moisture content (USDA Wood Handbook, coast Douglas-fir), as its limit stress. Graded lumber design values are much lower, so edit this for your grade and code.
